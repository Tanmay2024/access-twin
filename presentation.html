1. The Problem: The Indoor Accessibility Blindspot

Why conventional maps fail mobility-impaired individuals

Problem Statement

❌ Static Floor Plans

Conventional navigation apps stop at the building threshold. Indoor maps are typically static PDFs or static directories that assume all infrastructure is permanently operational.

⚠️ The "Broken Elevator" Trap

A wheelchair user travels across a large campus to an elevator, only to find an out-of-order sign. They are stranded with no knowledge of alternative accessible ramps or lifts.

👥 One Size Does Not Fit All

Mobility needs vary drastically: electric wheelchair users require strict step-free paths, while elderly visitors or injured students need minimized walking distances.

🔕 Lack of Incident Feedback

When unexpected blockages occur (luggage blocking a ramp, temporary maintenance), facility management lacks a real-time crowdsourcing feedback loop to warn visitors.

3. Technical Architecture

Standards-based spatial graph and client-side intelligence

Engineering
1

GeoJSON Graph

Spatial modeling of nodes (entrances, ramps, lift shafts, rooms) and edges with distance metrics and accessibility attributes (wheelchair_accessible: true/false).

2

Dijkstra Engine

Custom accessibility-filtered pathfinding that prunes blocked nodes and inaccessible edge transitions before computing shortest weighted paths.

3

SVG Dynamic Layer

Real-time vector rendering engine that overlays glowing directional trails, waypoint markers, and live GPS radar pulses directly over the blueprint.

🔄 Closed-Loop Incident Management

Visitor Report: Flags an unrecorded barrier on their path → Facility Admin View: Ticket logged in real-time → Verification: Admin verifies & updates status with 1 click → System Reroute: Campus routes update immediately.
